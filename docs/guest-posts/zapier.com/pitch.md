## Three topic options

### Option 1 (recommended): Your Zap is straining. Fix it, split it or replace it?
- Promise: a developer's rule for deciding what to do with a Zap that is slow, costly or breaking, with the cheaper fixes tried first.
- Bullets:
  - Four signals that a workflow is outgrowing a Zap (needs memory of earlier runs, long-running or heavy data work, failure that costs money, data you must keep in your own systems).
  - What to try inside Zapier first: split the Zap, move logic into a Code step, add error handlers and replay.
  - How to replace only the one piece that needs it and keep the rest in Zapier.
- Why Zapier readers care: it helps the person who built the Zap and now carries it, and it keeps most of the work in Zapier.
- What Waqas adds: [a real case where he rebuilt part of a Zapier or Make workflow in code, and what triggered it. Needs answers to Q1-Q4].
- Risk: Zapier may not want "leave Zapier" content. The framing must be "try Zapier's own fixes first". Not covered on zapier.com as far as I could find (searches 2026-10-20). Their Code by Zapier guide covers Code steps but not when to go beyond them.

### Option 2 (fallback if he has no Zap-to-code story): The exceptions page: write it before you build any automation
- Promise: a one-page list of everything that goes wrong in a process today, written before the first trigger is chosen.
- Bullets:
  - Interview the person who does the work, not the person who owns the process.
  - Record each exception: how often, who decides, what happens if it is missed.
  - Turn each exception into a path, a human handoff or a "not automated" decision.
- Why readers care: Zapier's process-mapping post (a tools roundup) only touches on branching and handoffs; an exceptions-first method is practical and role-neutral.
- What Waqas adds: [a real mapping session with a client: what the exceptions list contained and what it changed. Needs answers to Q5-Q8].
- Risk: lower distinctiveness; many agencies write about process mapping.

### Option 3: Handing an automation to someone else: owner, alert, fallback
- Promise: three things to write down on the day you hand a workflow to a colleague.
- Bullets: a named owner, an alert that reaches a person, a manual fallback when it is down.
- Why readers care: ops managers inherit Zaps they did not build.
- What Waqas adds: [a real handover that went wrong or right]. Needs Q9-Q10.
